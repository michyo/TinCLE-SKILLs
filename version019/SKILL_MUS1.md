# Music Genre Skill Sheet

## Eurobeat

If you need to compose a Eurobeat track, refer to the following guidelines.

### 1. Tempo & Key
- **BPM:** Strictly between 150 and 160.
- **Key:** Minor keys are mandatory (e.g., A minor, D minor, C minor) to create the signature "melancholy yet energetic" (Aishou) vibe.

### 2. Chord Progressions
- **Core Loops:**
  - `VI - VII - I` (e.g., F -> G -> Am) for maximum energy. Best for the Synth Riff and Chorus.
  - `VI - VII - III - VI` (e.g., F -> G -> Em -> Am) for a dramatic, emotional progression. Best for the Pre-Chorus (B-Melo).
- **Voicing:** Use open voicings. Avoid muddy low-mid chords. Keep the top notes melodic and bright.

### 3. Bassline (Crucial for Tracker/MML)
- **Pattern:** 16th-note octave jumps (Machine-gun bass).
- **MML Example:** `o2 a16 o3 a16 o2 a16 o3 a16` (Low -> High -> Low -> High repeated).
- **Tone:** Sawtooth wave with a quick decay/release for a tight, aggressive attack.

### 4. Drum Pattern
- **Kick:** 4-on-the-floor (solid quarter notes).
- **Snare/Clap:** Hard hit on the 2nd and 4th beats.
- **Hi-Hat:** 8th-note off-beats (Open hi-hat on the "and" of every beat).
- **Fills:** Rapid 16th-note snare rolls or synth tom fills at the end of every 8th bar. If an orchestra hit is available, place it on the 1st beat of a new section.

### 5. Song Structure & Lead Synth
- **Structure:** Intro -> Synth Riff (Hook) -> A-Melo (Verse) -> B-Melo (Pre-Chorus) -> Chorus -> Synth Riff -> Outro.
- **Lead Tone:** Bright, detuned Synth Brass (SuperSaw). It must play a highly catchy, fast melody that acts as the secondary vocal line.

### 6. Arrangement & Variation Ideas (To Avoid Repetition)
To ensure multiple generated Eurobeat tracks sound distinct, apply one or more of the following variations to your generation:

- **Bassline Variation:** Instead of strict octave jumps, use a "gallop" rhythm (one 8th note followed by two 16th notes) or use a steady 8th-note pedal point bass during the A-Melo to build tension.
- **Rhythm Breakdown:** Drop the kick drum entirely during the B-Melo (Pre-Chorus). Use a half-time snare or breakbeat pattern, then explode back into the full 4-on-the-floor beat for the Chorus.
- **Lead Instrument Swap:** Instead of always using Synth Brass for the main riff, substitute it with a high-pitched Square wave with heavy glide/portamento, or a rapid 16th-note Trance-style Arpeggio.
- **Modulation (Key Change):** Shift the key up by +1 or +2 semitones (+1 or +2 in note pitch) for the final repetition of the Chorus to artificially boost the climax.
- **Intro Variation:** Start the song with only the Kick and the acapella/melody, or start with a dramatic, slow pad sound before a drum roll brings in the main 155 BPM tempo.

## Soca (Brazilian / Carnival Dance Crossover)

If you need to compose an uptempo, high-energy Soca or Brazilian-crossover dance track (e.g., Axé, Pagodão Baiano, Merenhouse), refer to the following guidelines. The goal is to recreate the "Carnival Mind"—the massive, sweaty, jump-up energy of street parades in Bahia (Salvador) and Trinidad.

### 1. Tempo & Vibe
- **BPM:** Usually between 125 and 135 (Merenhouse/Pagodão feel), or up to 140-150 for extreme Jump-up Soca.
- **Vibe:** Festive, hypnotic, and highly physical. The track must make the listener want to jump up and down.

### 2. Rhythm & The "3+3+2" (Tresillo) Core
The rhythm is the absolute most important element. It relies on African-derived syncopation mixed with a driving 2/4 feel.
- **The 3+3+2 Pattern:** The primary percussion and bass often play a 16th-note syncopation pattern grouping: [3 + 3 + 2].
  - *Grid (16th notes):* `[ X . . X . . X . ]`
- **Kick:** A heavy 4-on-the-floor, OR a strong 2-beat drive (Kick on beats 1 and 3, or heavily accenting the downbeats) to ground the jumping motion.
- **Percussion (Crucial):** Mix electronic drums with acoustic/Latin elements. Layer crisp, acoustic snare snaps, timbales, congas, or cuica over the beat. The snare/percussion should carry the syncopated 3+3+2 drive.

### 3. Bassline (Heavy & Bouncy)
- **Role:** The bass provides the modern crossover element (Caribbean Dance Tech / Reggaeton / Favela sound).
- **Tone:** Use a heavy Sub-bass (808 style) or a thick, rolling synth bass (FM bass).
- **Pattern:** The bass should intertwine tightly with the percussion. It often plays the Tresillo rhythm or aggressively hits the off-beats to create an undulating, bouncy groove. Keep the notes short and punchy to leave room for the heavy Kick.

### 4. Chords & Harmony
- **Progression:** Keep it extremely simple and repetitive. 2-bar or 4-bar vamps are ideal (e.g., `i - iv - V` or strictly toggling between two chords like `i` and `VII`).
- **Rhythm:** Chords (played by piano, synth brass, or accordion) should play rhythmic stabs, often hitting the upbeat (ska/reggae style) or following the percussion syncopation.

### 5. Instrumentation & Leads
- **Lead Sounds:** Bright Synth Brass, traditional Horn Sections, Accordion, or high-pitched Plucks (Marimba/Steelpan style).
- **Hype Elements:** If the tracker supports noise/effects, use whistle sounds (Samba whistle/Apito) on the beat to simulate a street carnival.

### 6. Arrangement & Variation Ideas (To Avoid Repetition)
To ensure multiple generated tracks sound distinct, apply these variations:

- **The "Drop" (Percussion & Bass Only):** For a section (like the B-Melo/Pre-Chorus), mute the chords and lead melody entirely. Let only the heavy sub-bass, kick, and complex percussion groove carry the track. This is a classic Pagodão technique.
- **Rhythm Shift (Double-time/Half-time feel):** Introduce rapid 16th-note hi-hats (Trap/Dancehall style) for one section, then switch back to an open hi-hat tropical groove for the Chorus.
- **Call and Response:** Write the melody as a short, aggressive phrase followed by a gap, simulating a crowd shouting back. (e.g., Lead brass plays a 2-beat riff, then silence for 2 beats).
- **Instrument Swap:** Alternate the main riff between an acoustic-sounding instrument (like Accordion or Steelpan) in the Intro, and a massive synthesized Brass patch in the Chorus.
